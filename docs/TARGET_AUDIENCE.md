# Target Audience

## Primary consumer audience
Adults with recurring financial and administrative obligations.

The broad addressable user can have:
- Bills
- Subscriptions
- Contracts
- Purchases
- Insurance
- Warranties
- Travel bookings
- IDs
- Renewals
- Utilities
- Memberships
- Appointments
- Government/admin obligations

## Best initial consumer segment
Start with digitally active adults who:
- Receive most bills/renewals by email
- Have multiple subscriptions or recurring services
- Are comfortable uploading/forwarding documents
- Feel they may be overpaying or missing refund/renewal opportunities
- Value convenience enough to pay for ongoing protection

Potential early adopter profiles:
- Young professionals
- Frequent online shoppers
- Subscription-heavy households
- People managing multiple utilities/services
- Frequent travelers
- Busy parents/households

## Secondary prosumer segments
- Freelancers
- Consultants
- Creators
- Contractors
- Small landlords
- Small business owners

These users have more frequent financial/admin pain and may accept higher pricing.

## Household expansion
The household can become a stronger retention unit than an individual.

Possible household entities:
- Spouse/partner
- Children
- Vehicles
- Property
- Insurance
- Passports/visas
- School documents
- Warranties
- Pets
- Shared subscriptions

## SMB target
Businesses that do not have dedicated procurement/compliance/admin teams but still manage:
- Contracts
- Vendor renewals
- Insurance
- Software spend
- Licenses
- Employee certifications
- Invoice errors
- Deadlines

## Enterprise target
Larger organizations that need:
- Obligation monitoring
- Spend/renewal protection
- Governance
- Approval workflows
- Auditability
- Cross-department visibility

## Core segmentation principle
Initial acquisition messaging should focus on a specific pain, not the broad life-admin vision.

Preferred launch message:

> Stop losing money without realizing it.
