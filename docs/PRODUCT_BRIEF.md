# Product Brief — Watchtower

## One-line concept
Watchtower is an AI-powered personal protection agent that watches for things that can quietly cost users money, time, or trouble, then helps handle them before they become problems.

## Initial wedge
Start with the narrow promise:

> Stop me losing money without realizing it.

Watchtower should identify and eventually act on issues such as:
- Subscription price increases
- Forgotten subscriptions
- Duplicate charges
- Refunds that were approved but never received
- Free trials ending
- Contract renewals and cancellation deadlines
- Warranty and return-window expiries
- Insurance renewals
- Incorrect bills
- Unexplained recurring charges
- Compensation/refund opportunities
- Credits or entitlements the user has not claimed

## Long-term vision
Expand from money protection into a broader personal administrative agent that continuously monitors obligations, deadlines, documents, purchases, contracts, renewals, and life-admin tasks.

Long-term promise:

> Just handle it.

## Why this should exist
People routinely lose money or suffer avoidable stress because:
- Important information is buried in emails, contracts, receipts, and notices.
- Administrative tasks are boring and easy to postpone.
- Deadlines are forgotten.
- Price increases and renewal terms are overlooked.
- Refunds, credits, warranties, and entitlements are not pursued.
- Existing tools mostly remind, summarize, or explain rather than complete the outcome.

## Core agent loop
The product should evolve toward:

Detect -> Understand -> Decide -> Act -> Verify -> Escalate

Most AI assistants stop at understanding or recommending. Watchtower should eventually deliver verified outcomes.

## Product surface
The user should not need to “manage AI agents.” The product should feel like a guardian that runs quietly in the background.

Possible inputs:
- Paste text
- Upload PDF/image/email screenshot
- Forward email
- Gmail/Outlook connection
- Calendar connection
- Later: bank/card data and additional services

Possible outputs:
- Cost exposure
- Deadline alerts
- Recommendations
- Watchlist items
- Drafts and actions
- Verified resolution
- Money saved/protected

## Platform expansion
1. Personal
2. Household / Family
3. Small Business
4. Enterprise

The architecture should remain shared across all tiers.

## Enterprise direction
Enterprise value proposition:

> Protect the organization from missed money, deadlines, and obligations.

Potential enterprise use cases:
- Vendor renewals
- Software/SaaS contracts
- Insurance renewals
- Licenses and certifications
- Regulatory deadlines
- SLA credits
- Supplier contract obligations
- Purchase order issues
- Invoice errors
- Warranty claims
- Employee credential expiries

Enterprise differentiation should come from:
- SSO
- RBAC
- Approval workflows
- Policies
- Audit logs
- Data residency
- API
- Admin reporting
- Department ownership
- Retention policies
- Integrations

## North-star value metric
The product should make value visible through a metric such as:

> Money protected this year

Supporting categories:
- Avoided costs
- Money recovered
- Price increases prevented
- Credits claimed
- Time saved
